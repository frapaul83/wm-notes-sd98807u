Working Diagnosis: #6

A one-page map of the theory first laid out in early October 2026: where mast-cell / immune disease, gut inflammation, dietary sensitivities and variegate porphyria cross paths, and how that crossing shows up as whole-body edema, kidney retention and heart-rate changes. Working notes for discussion with the treating physician; not a formal diagnosis.

Read this first

Causation is not settled. It is open whether porphyria set off the gut problem and the loop, or whether immune / mast-cell disease started something that porphyria then amplified, or some mix of the two. What is known is that porphyria, gut inflammation and edema are connected. Each piece is being worked on separately. The arrows below show a proposed sequence, not an established direction.

“#6” is the name for the single place where everything crosses: real mast-cell / immune disease, gut inflammation, dietary sensitivities and porphyria. Each one feeds the others, and together they turn a local gut problem into a systemic one (swelling throughout the body, kidneys holding water, heart rate). It is not one step in a chain; it is the junction the whole picture runs through.

00What #6 means

  • Mast-cell / immune disease (mastocytosis history) releases histamine and cytokines, including from the gut wall.
  • Gut inflammation stalls motility, holds fluid in the gut and sends inflammatory signals into the body.
  • Dietary sensitivities (fructans and other offenders) set off the gut and mast cells; removing them brings motility back, starts diuresis and lowers inflammation.
  • Porphyria is triggered by inflammation, stalls the gut through its autonomic effects, and may also add to water retention (see the note on ADH below).
  • Where they cross: whole-body edema, kidneys holding on, heart-rate rises, and a weight that never resets.

Also worth knowing: acute porphyria attacks, VP included, are a recognised cause of inappropriate ADH (SIADH), which holds water and can lower blood sodium. Secondary adrenal insufficiency, which is being considered separately, can produce a similar picture; porphyria-driven ADH is noted here as one possible mechanism, not the only one. Included for awareness, not as a leading explanation. Serum sodium with serum and urine osmolality, drawn while swollen and ideally off diuretics, would show whether it is playing a part.

02What the history shows

Gut and food

  • Regurgitation was a release valve. Before April it offloaded part of each meal; it stopped when the flare began, and the full load has gone through since, with rapid gastric emptying.
  • Less food, fewer symptoms. No meals did best, small frequent meals next, large meals worst. Clean, simple diets improve things.
  • Avoiding fructans and other offenders does more than restore motility. When they are removed, motility returns, diuresis begins and inflammation starts to come down: one gut input, several whole-body responses.
  • Prokinetics fail during stalls (prucalopride, linaclotide), which suggests the stall is not simply a motility defect.
  • Heart rate can rise within 10–30 minutes of eating. That is too fast for fermentation. It fits fast emptying and early dumping: fluid pulled into the gut, blood pooling in the gut circulation, effective volume drops.

Fluid and weight

  • Swelling is whole-body: face, under the chin, arms and wrists, belly, calves, shins and ankles. It is not limited to dependent areas.
  • Release episodes recur without a diuretic: nights of 1 L+ urine ahead of intake, heart rate settles, heavy bowel movement and gas.
  • The 50+ lb gained since April has never reset. Most plausibly a large share is fluid; fat would need a ~175,000 kcal surplus on 800–1,600 kcal/day (tirzepatide). Steroid effect and bowel contents add some.
  • Each release stops short of baseline, so the next load starts from a higher fluid level.
  • The tension: the gut does best with less food, but VP needs a carbohydrate floor (200 g/day minimum, 260–280 g target). A dextrose trial (sugar with almost no food bulk) could separate the two needs.

07Where it stands

  • Combined course (prednisone, loop + thiazide diuretic, rifaximin): swelling came down throughout the body and overnight heart rate fell. This fits the model, but with several treatments at once it cannot show which piece drives which.
  • Prednisone is being tapered in 10 mg steps every 5 days; a low standing dose may continue for a possible secondary adrenal insufficiency unrelated to the flare.
  • Diuretics continue for now, then are reviewed together with cetirizine.
  • The taper is the next natural test: what returns, and in what order, as each layer is lifted.
  • At baseline: a new standing protocol, possibly with regular weigh-ins.
